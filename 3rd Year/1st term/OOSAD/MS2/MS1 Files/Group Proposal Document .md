1. **Problem Statement**  
   This section presents the challenges MMDC students experience when finding compatible groupmates for subject-based academic projects. It focuses on the difficulties caused by the fully online learning environment, differences in IT majors, schedules, employment commitments, and preferred working or study styles, as well as the impact these challenges have on the initial group-formation process.  
     
   1. **Background**  
        
      Mapúa Malayan Digital College (MMDC) is a fully online college environment where students primarily complete their academic activities through digital platforms. MMDC offers Bachelor of Science in Information Technology programs with majors in Software Development, Data Analytics, Network & Cybersecurity, Marketing Technology, and AI-Powered Product Development.  
        
      The learning environment requires students to manage much of their academic work independently. Each subject typically has one synchronous class per week, while students select their preferred class schedule before the semester begins. Students also have an opportunity to attend a weekly mentoring session with their mentor for each subject.  
        
      MMDC uses several digital platforms to support its learning process. Google Meet is used for synchronous classes, while Google Workspace supports collaboration and other academic activities. Coursera provides asynchronous learning materials, while MyCamu is used for submitting major graded requirements.  
        
      A significant part of the academic structure involves group-based projects. In many subjects, students need to form a group before proceeding to the major project milestones. Since students have different courses, schedules, study preferences, and approaches to collaboration, forming a suitable group can become challenging.

   2. **Description of the Problem**  
        
      The specific problem addressed by this project is the **difficulty MMDC students experience when finding compatible groupmates** for subject-based academic projects.  
        
      Because MMDC is a fully online learning environment, students may have limited opportunities to interact with other classmates outside their scheduled synchronous sessions. Students may know only a small number of people taking the same subject, while potential groupmates may have different IT majors, schedules, work commitments, skill sets, or preferred ways of collaborating.  
        
      The challenge is particularly relevant during the **initial group-formation stage** of a subject, when students are expected to form their project groups before beginning Milestone 1\. Students need to identify classmates who are taking the same subject and are available and willing to collaborate on the project.  
        
      Finding suitable groupmates can be difficult when students rely on informal communication channels or personal connections. A student may need to ask multiple classmates individually whether they are still looking for a group, whether their schedules are compatible, and whether they are comfortable with a particular working arrangement.  
        
      Another factor is that many **students may also be working while studying**. Working students can have fixed employment schedules or limited availability outside working hours. As a result, two students taking the same subject may still have difficulty collaborating if their available schedules do not overlap.  
        
      For example, a student may be available primarily during evenings because of work, while another student may prefer afternoon collaboration. Similarly, students from different IT majors may have different academic strengths or project preferences that could affect how they work together.  
        
      Therefore, students need a more structured way to discover potential groupmates based on relevant factors such as subject, **IT major, availability, employment status and work schedule, and preferred working or study style.**

    


   3. **Impact of the Problem**  
        
      Difficulty in finding compatible groupmates can result in delays during the initial group-formation stage of a subject. Students may spend considerable time searching for available classmates instead of beginning to organize their project.  
        
      The problem can also result in groups being formed primarily based on who is immediately available rather than considering factors that may affect collaboration. Differences in schedules, employment commitments, academic backgrounds, technical interests, and working styles can make coordination more difficult once the project begins.  
        
      The primary stakeholders affected are MMDC students, particularly students who have difficulty identifying classmates taking the same subject and finding groupmates whose schedules and working arrangements are compatible with their own.  
        
      Working students may experience an additional challenge because their employment schedules can restrict the times when they are able to participate in group meetings and collaborative activities.  
        
      In the short term, these challenges can lead to additional communication, difficulty coordinating schedules, and delays in establishing a project group. In the longer term, repeated difficulties with group formation can make collaborative academic work more challenging for students in a fully online learning environment.

2. **Objectives**  
   This section defines the goals and specific objectives of StudySync in providing MMDC students with a structured way to find potential groupmates for subject-based academic projects. It outlines the intended system capabilities, including profile creation, groupmate matching, requests, project group formation, notifications, and basic administration.  
     
   1. **Project Goals**

      The main goal of StudySync is to provide MMDC students with a centralized platform for finding compatible groupmates for subject-based academic projects.

      

      The system aims to make group formation more organized by allowing students to identify potential groupmates based on relevant academic and collaboration-related information, such as their subject, IT major, availability, employment status, work schedule when applicable, and preferred study or working style.

      

      If these goals are achieved, students should be able to spend less time manually searching for groupmates and have a more structured way of forming suitable project teams

      

   2. **Specific Objectives**  
        
      The project aims to:

1. Allow students to create a study and collaboration profile containing relevant information such as their IT major, subjects, availability, employment status, work schedule when applicable, and preferred working or study style.  
2. Allow students to identify potential groupmates who are taking the same subject and have compatible availability, employment schedules when applicable, or collaboration preferences.  
3. Provide a matching mechanism that recommends potential groupmates based on selected compatibility criteria.  
4. Allow students to send and manage groupmate requests, including accepting or declining requests.  
5. Allow students to organize a project group after compatible students agree to work together.  
6. Provide notifications or status updates for relevant matching and group-formation activities.  
7. Provide administrators with basic management capabilities for maintaining system information and handling user-related concerns.

   

   The objectives will be considered achieved when the proposed system can demonstrate the complete process from student profile creation → potential groupmate discovery → matching → request → acceptance → group formation.

   

3. **Proposed Solution**  
   This section presents StudySync as an online groupmate-matching system designed for the MMDC learning environment. It describes how students can create collaboration profiles, identify potential groupmates based on relevant criteria, send groupmate requests, and form project groups, while keeping the system separate from existing MMDC platforms such as Coursera, MyCamu, Google Meet, and Google Workspace.  
     
   1. **Solution Overview**

      The proposed solution is StudySync, an online groupmate-matching system designed specifically for the MMDC learning environment.

      

      The system will allow students to create a profile containing information relevant to academic collaboration. Students can indicate their IT major, enrolled subjects, available schedules, employment status, work schedule when applicable, and preferred study or working style.

      

      The system will then use these criteria to identify potential groupmates taking the same subject and having compatible characteristics, particularly in terms of availability and collaboration preferences.

      

      A simplified process would be:

      

      Student Registration

      ↓

      Create Collaboration Profile

      ↓

      Select Subject

      ↓

      Set Availability & Collaboration Preferences

      ↓

      View Potential Groupmates

      ↓

      Send Groupmate Request

      ↓

      Accept / Decline Request

      ↓

      Form Project Group

      

      The system is intended to support the initial group-formation stage of MMDC subject projects. It is not intended to replace the school's existing learning platforms such as Coursera, MyCamu, Google Meet, or Google Workspace.

      

      

      **Main Features**

      

      **Student Profile**

      

* Students can maintain information relevant to finding compatible groupmates.


  **Subject Selection**


* Students can identify the subject for which they are looking for groupmates.


  **Availability**


* Students can specify their preferred days or times for collaboration.


  


  

  **Availability & Employment**

* Students can specify whether they are a working student or full-time student and provide their typical work schedule when applicable.


  **Study/Working Style**


* Students can indicate preferences such as independent work, frequent collaboration, structured meetings, or other predefined working preferences.


  **Groupmate Matching**


* The system identifies potential students based on shared subject enrollment and selected compatibility criteria such as IT major, availability, employment status and work schedule when applicable, and collaboration preferences.


  **Groupmate Requests**


* Students can send, accept, or decline requests to collaborate.


  **Project Group Formation**


* Students who agree to collaborate can establish a project group within the system.


  


  **Notifications**


* Users can receive updates about matching results and groupmate requests.


  2. **Benefits of the Solution**

     *StudySync will provide students with a more organized approach to finding groupmates. Instead of relying primarily on personal connections or manually searching through different communication channels, students can use one system to identify classmates who are taking the same subject and have compatible collaboration preferences.*

     

     *The system can reduce the time and effort required to find potential groupmates and make the group-formation process more structured.*

     

     *Students may also benefit from forming groups based on more than simply knowing one another. Considering factors such as major, availability, and working style can help students identify people whose circumstances are more compatible with their own.*

     

     *For MMDC students, the proposed system can support the collaborative nature of project-based learning while remaining separate from the school's existing systems for learning materials, meetings, and submissions.*

It establishes the system's capabilities for account management, collaboration profiles, groupmate matching, requests, project group formation, notifications, and administration, while defining the technical, privacy, development, and platform boundaries of the project.

      The proposed system will provide the following functionalities:  
        
      **Student Account Management**

* Log in and log out  
* Manage basic profile information


  

  **Collaboration Profile**

* Select their IT major  
* Select relevant subjects  
* Set their available schedule  
* Specify employment status: working student or full-time student  
* Provide their typical work schedule if they are a working student  
* Specify study/working preferences  
* Update their profile


  **Groupmate Matching**

  The system can:

* Identify students taking the same subject  
* Compare selected compatibility criteria  
* Display potential groupmates  
* Allow students to view relevant profile information

  **Groupmate Requests**

  Students can:

* Send a groupmate request  
* Receive requests  
* Accept requests  
* Decline requests  
* View request status

  **Project Group Formation**

  Students can:

* Create a project group  
* Invite/accept members  
* View group members  
* **View basic group information**

  **Notifications**

  The system can notify students about:

* New groupmate requests  
* Accepted/declined requests  
* Group invitations  
* Relevant group updates


  **Administration**

  Administrators can:

* Manage student accounts  
* Manage subjects  
* Manage available system information

  2. **Non-Functional Requirements**  
       
     **Usability**  
     The system should provide a simple and intuitive interface that can be easily understood by students who regularly use online learning platforms.  
       
     **Performance**  
     The system should return matching results and user actions within a reasonable response time under normal usage conditions.  
       
     **Security**  
     Student account information should be protected through appropriate authentication and access controls. Users should only be able to modify information associated with their own accounts.  
       
       
     **Reliability**  
     The system should maintain consistent information regarding student profiles, matching requests, and group membership.  
       
       
       
     **Compatibility**  
     The proposed system should be designed as a web-based platform that can be accessed using common desktop and mobile web browsers.

  3. **Assumptions and Constraints**  
     

     **Assumptions**  
     The project assumes that:  
       
* Students provide accurate information about their major, subjects, availability, employment status, work schedule when applicable, and collaboration preferences.  
* Students are willing to create profiles and use the system to find potential groupmates.  
* Students use the system primarily for forming groups for academic projects.  
* The system will initially focus on MMDC students and subjects.  
* Matching recommendations will be based on predefined compatibility criteria rather than an advanced AI recommendation system.


  **Constraints**

  The project may be constrained by:

* Limited development time within the academic term.  
* Limited access to actual MMDC student enrollment and schedule data.  
* The need to protect student information and privacy.  
* The project team's available technical resources.  
* The system's dependence on students entering accurate information.  
* The need to avoid duplicating or replacing existing MMDC platforms and services.

5. **Conclusion**  
   

   MMDC's fully online learning environment provides students with flexibility in attending classes and completing their academic requirements, but it can also make forming project groups more difficult. Students may need to find classmates who share the same subject while also considering differences in IT majors, schedules, employment commitments, and collaboration preferences.  
     
     
   The proposed StudySync: MMDC Student Groupmate Matching System addresses this problem by providing a centralized platform where students can create collaboration profiles, identify potential groupmates, send requests, and form project groups.  
     
   By organizing the group-formation process around relevant compatibility factors such as subject enrollment, IT major, availability, employment status and work schedule when applicable, and collaboration preferences, the system aims to reduce the time and effort students spend searching for groupmates and provide a more structured approach to academic collaboration.  
     
   The proposed system is also intentionally limited to the group-formation and initial collaboration stage. It does not attempt to replace Coursera, MyCamu, Google Meet, Google Workspace, or other existing MMDC platforms. This controlled scope makes the project feasible while still providing sufficient functionality and interactions for the OOSAD project.
